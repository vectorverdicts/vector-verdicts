A German radio message from World War One sat unsolved for over a century. Then GPT-6 Astra cracked it. The message used ADFGVX, a notoriously punishing field cipher. Human codebreakers cracked hundreds in 1918, but a handful permanently resisted. Astra broke through. Decoded, the telegram warned that a British cruiser had reached Sevastopol, with an allied squadron following behind. British Admiralty logs verify it: HMS Canterbury was right there. But Astra didn't reinvent cryptanalysis. It simply cycled historical German military keys until one fit, the standard word for troop movement. Even then, the output hedged, coughing up an uncertain date that required human historians to confirm against ship logs. A breakthrough? Machine patience and a lucky dictionary hit.
