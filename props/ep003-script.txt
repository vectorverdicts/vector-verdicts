An encrypted German radio message from World War One sat unsolved for over a hundred years. GPT-6 Astra from OpenAI cracked it. The message used a field cipher called ADFGVX and dated from November 1918. Human codebreakers had already solved hundreds of these; a handful always resisted. Astra broke through. Decoded, it said an English cruiser had reached Sevastopol, and an allied squadron followed on the twenty-sixth. Ship logs line up. The HMS Canterbury arrived in late November, with the squadron right behind. But Astra didn't crack anything by force. It flipped through known German keys until one worked, the word for troop movement. Even then, its own plaintext hedged, carrying an uncertain digit. So is this a breakthrough? Kind of. More like machine patience and one lucky guess, checked against an archive. Impressive to watch. Not destiny. Vector Verdicts.
